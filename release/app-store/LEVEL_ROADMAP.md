# EXCEPTION HUMAN launch levels and expansion roadmap

October 25, 2026. Production design supporting the [App Store plan](../../APP_STORE_DEPLOYMENT_PLAN.md). Preserve the existing [200-server catalog](../../campaign/CATALOG.md) and stable IDs. Localhost's twenty long routes exist; the other nine centers still require authored layouts, assets, commentary, and validation.

## Direction for the first release

Make Localhost a complete first game with a beginning, rising disagreement, a difficult final shift, and a proper escape. Offer the twenty existing long servers at launch. Do not wait for 180 additional layouts before discovering whether phone players enjoy the loop.

Keep move, jump, and nearest-object Rewrite as the entire action vocabulary. The AI's predictions, forms, deleted support, receipts, maintenance systems, and cosmetic memory failures create the variety. Teach one decision, allow a confident repeat, then expose the AI's flawed assumption. A new level needs a memorable decision or situation beyond a higher obstacle count.

The four chapters follow **teach → develop → twist → remix → incident**. Each five-level chapter offers a clear stopping point. Every fifth server is an authored set piece, not a new control scheme. Optional raised routes reward mastery; the cautious route must remain completable. Stable maintenance bays and Pause provide real thinking time.

Keep the current automated clean-run benchmarks, ranging from roughly 76 seconds to 181 seconds and 302 seconds for the finale, as technical references. They exclude death-reading time and do not predict a beginner's session. Use real phone sessions to judge repetition, fatigue, and whether a long route earns its length. [Current timing and acts](../../DATA_CENTER_CAMPAIGN.md#october-23-twenty-long-localhost-rebuilds).

## Production order for the remaining 180 levels

1. Validate Localhost's phone pacing and the content pipeline with one small permanent challenge pack.
2. Build Cache Management 01 to 05 as the first expansion chapter. Use the three existing Cache prototypes as comparison material, not finished campaign servers.
3. Complete Cache's remaining chapters before awarding its twenty-clear badge. Ship chapters only when each is a satisfying unit; show clearly when a later chapter is not available.
4. Build Prediction Services and Permission Control after evidence that the existing forecast/rewrite vocabulary still produces fresh choices.
5. Continue through Garbage Collection, Merge Conflict, Context Storage, and Transparency Division with a fresh mechanic/readability review at each world boundary.
6. Finish Billing Infrastructure and Production when the core campaign is sustainable to author and test. Do not commit to a monthly world or public completion date for all 200 slots now.

An initial five-level expansion might take three to five weeks after tooling stabilizes, assuming one lead and supporting art/audio/QA time. This is a capacity hypothesis to replace with measured authoring time after the first chapter. Larger production staffing can change the cadence; safety and fun gates stay the same.

## A repeatable level design brief

Every level needs a short design card covering:

- The AI's concrete wrong claim and the player's observable counterplay.
- Three to five named acts, a quiet introduction, a changed decision, and a satisfying exit.
- A cautious route, an optional mastery route where appropriate, a safe recovery, and protected rests.
- The exact physical failure causes and the correct commentary families.
- At least 12 unique themed backgrounds per future level; target 24 per campaign server, matching Localhost's current authoring depth.
- Entry, success, incident, recovery, and loss copy; persistent rotation and no jokes masquerading as physical markers.
- A clean-run benchmark, a fresh-player observation, a supported-phone framing check, and replay/reward eligibility.
- Art/audio source and rights, content dependencies, mechanic requirements, and a human signoff.

Check progression as a chapter, not just five isolated routes. The player should feel more capable by the fifth server even while the AI becomes more visibly defensive. Avoid escalating route length, movement speed, and simultaneous hazards together.

## Rewards and mastery

Preserve the current 10-Token first-clear bonus and best-remaining-credit improvement rules. Preserve the three starter stickers, two style badges, fixed prices, and permanent ownership. The first sticker remains affordable after the first real campaign clear. [Existing economy](../../CREDITS_AND_UNRELIABLE_HINTS.md#token-bank-stickers-and-data-center-badges).

A center badge requires all twenty campaign clears, regardless of death count or efficiency. Never sell an earned badge or make its requirements depend on ad views. Keep lifetime accomplishments separate from the spendable wallet. Replays should reward mastery without requiring daily grinding.

For additional badges, begin with a small set of allowlisted accomplishments such as a specific permanent incident clear or completion of a seasonal set. Avoid achievements that punish accessibility settings, require perfect runs before a player understands a trap, or count a practice completion as campaign progress. New reward-rule code requires an app update.

## Seasonal incident format

Start with at most one modest featured event per quarter after operations stabilize. A season contains three to five authored challenges using supported mechanics, roughly 36 to 120 new themed background lines depending on its size, a paper treatment, a short music variation, a clear receipt, and one earned commemorative badge.

Possible themes are **Quarterly Compliance**, **Holiday Change Freeze**, **Spring Cleanup**, and **Annual Performance Review**. Keep the jokes understandable without technical knowledge. Seasonal humor changes the AI's excuses; it does not falsify real timers, hitboxes, reward balances, or privacy controls.

Keep earned rewards and an archive route after the feature window. Avoid a required login streak, expiring paid pass, or punishment for missing a date. Feature dates are a discovery tool, not a reason to destroy a player's access.

## Fun review before promotion

Watch a fresh player without coaching beyond the intended onboarding. Record the real point of confusion, readable warning, chosen response, perceived fairness, and willingness to retry. Separately watch a practiced player take the optional route. Both should have a clear reason to enjoy the level.

Promote content only when the ordinary-control route works, protected waits remain safe, touch inputs are comfortable, and the joke follows what actually happened. Automated route proof, authored content count, and a familiar developer's successful run do not establish that the level is fun.
